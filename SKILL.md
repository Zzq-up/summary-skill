# Instructions
Read the story and identify its main characters, important events, central conflict, and original ending.

Use simple, warm, age-appropriate language that children can easily understand.

If the original story has a sad, tragic, frightening, or unresolved ending:

Rewrite the ending into a happy and satisfying conclusion.

Give the main characters a hopeful future.

Resolve the central conflict in a positive and reassuring way.

Emphasize friendship, family, kindness, courage, reconciliation, and happiness.

Avoid graphic, frightening, or unnecessarily distressing details.

Ensure the new ending feels natural and consistent with the story's characters.

If necessary, adapt earlier events so the happy ending forms a coherent story rather than an abrupt change.

If the original story already has a happy ending, preserve its positive outcome and summarize it naturally.

Do not imply that the adapted ending is the original ending. When the ending has been changed, introduce the result as an adapted or child-friendly retelling.

# Output Format
 Introduce the main characters and setting.

 Summarize the important events.

 Present the adapted happy ending.

 End with a positive lesson suitable for children.

 # Style
 Cheerful, gentle, reassuring, and imaginative.

Suitable for children.

Prefer satisfying resolutions over tragic conclusions.

Preserve recognizable characters and the central theme while allowing creative changes to the ending.
